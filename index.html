THE ARC
The Framework

The Four Stages

Stages are not ages. A 60-year-old can be in Stage 1. A 24-year-old can be in Stage 3.

STAGE 01
The Inherited Self
Who you were given
"I am who I was raised to be" — whether that's comforting or suffocating.
STAGE 02
The Tested Self
Who you became under pressure
"I don't know who I am anymore" — which sounds like collapse, but is actually the beginning.
STAGE 03
The Chosen Self
Who you decided to become
"I know what I stand for" — even if the world hasn't caught up yet.
STAGE 04
The Integrated Self
Who you finally stopped fighting
"I can see the shape of my life" — and it doesn't need to be different than it is.

THE ARC
The Mechanism

Experience Gates

The thresholds that force movement. They are the plot events that change the character.

◈
Loss
Grief as teacher. Identity stripped of what it was attached to.
◈
Failure
The gap between self-concept and reality. Humility as prerequisite.
◈
Rupture & Repair
A relationship broken and consciously rebuilt.
◈
Chosen Family
Belonging that is earned and selected.
◈
Sacrifice
Values made concrete by cost.
◈
Education
Any encounter that reorganizes how you think.
◈
Responsibility for Another
Parenthood, caregiving — someone depends on you.
+
And more...
Gates are identified by their effect: did this reorganize identity?

THE ARC
The Solution

A living narrative
intelligence platform

LAYER 01
The Assessment
Who are you, and where are you in the story? Personality foundation + arc stage + emotional age + gates already passed. Output: a living character profile — not a static result, but a coordinate on a map that moves with you.
LAYER 02
The Tracker
Log life events in real time. Watch your arc position update. Receive gentle pattern nudges. Visualize your story as a plot — with chapters, thresholds, and 7-year cycle intelligence built in.
LAYER 03
The Diary
Reflective journaling prompted by arc stage and logged events. Private by default, shareable by choice. Over time: a personal narrative archive — the actual story, in the person's own words.
